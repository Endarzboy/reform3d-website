Research overview

Abstract

Evaluation of 3D medical foundation models is typically dominated by downstream task scores, which entangle representation quality with adaptation choices. This is especially critical in volumetric imaging, where spacing and anisotropy can substantially alter image statistics while the anatomy itself remains unchanged.

We therefore introduce REFORM-3D, a Representation-centric Evaluation FramewORk for benchmarking frozen 3D medical vision foundation Models across three questions: Acquisition Robustness, asking whether features remain stable when spacing varies while anatomy is fixed; Cross-Modal Anatomical Alignment, asking whether labeled anatomical structures remain recoverable across modalities under balanced bidirectional retrieval; and Low-Shot Anatomical Transfer, asking whether organ identity remains recoverable from limited labeled support under case-disjoint evaluation.

These are also pretraining questions, because what a 3D encoder learns depends on how volumes are geometrically presented during self-supervised pretraining. We further introduce Med3DINO encoder family, pretrained on 62.6K unlabeled MR, CT, PET, CTA, and CBCT volumes under three explicit geometric assumptions: isotropic normalization, voxel-relative sampling, and spacing-aware resampling.

Across Med3DINO and heterogeneous external encoders, REFORM-3D reveals module-specific strengths rather than a single model ordering. Within the controlled Med3DINO comparison, voxel-relative sampling yields lower embedding drift, whereas spacing-aware resampling favors anatomical transfer; semantic-transfer advantages depend on the dataset and feature type.

Evaluation protocol

Frozen encoders. Complementary measurements.

REFORM-3D examines representation quality on KiTS23 and MMWHS. Each module measures a distinct property, with results reported separately rather than combined into a single ranking.

Datasets and primary evaluation metrics
Module Dataset Measurements
Spacing sensitivity KiTS23 Embedding drift, CKA, matched semantic transfer
Cross-modal alignment MMWHS CT–MR retrieval: mAP, Top-1, Top-5
Anatomical generalization MMWHS Centroid and probe balanced accuracy, adaptation gain

Encoders remain frozen throughout evaluation. Low-shot probes fit labeled readouts using case-disjoint support and query sets. Configure checkpoints, feature readouts, and seeds explicitly.

Key findings

Representation quality has multiple dimensions.

Stability and semantic usefulness can differ.

The manuscript’s controlled spacing experiments show that embedding drift, feature-space structure, and semantic transfer provide complementary evidence. Low drift alone does not establish robust anatomical representations.

Pretraining geometry shapes anatomical recoverability.

Within the controlled Med3DINO comparison, spacing-aware pretraining achieves the highest CT–MR retrieval scores and low-shot probe balanced accuracy on TotalSegmentator CT–MR, MMWHS, and AMOS22 with both CLS and GAP features.

Encoder strengths depend on the evaluation setting.

External encoders exhibit different strengths across datasets and modules. These comparisons support evaluating each property separately when selecting an encoder for a particular task.

These findings summarize the manuscript experiments. Controlled spacing changes are geometric stress tests; they do not reproduce the full variability of clinical acquisition. External encoders provide contextual comparisons, while Med3DINO variants form the controlled geometric-policy comparison.

From inputs to evidence

Configure. Check. Evaluate.

Start with Python 3.11 on Linux and a PyTorch build compatible with your CUDA installation. Install REFORM-3D, then edit the dataset and checkpoint paths in the example YAML.

The MMWHS example evaluates cross-modal retrieval and low-shot recognition. Use the KiTS23 example for controlled spacing evaluation.
